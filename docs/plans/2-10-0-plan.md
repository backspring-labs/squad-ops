# 2.10 plan — a supervised Campaign improves a playable Roblox game

**Status: DRAFT, revision 1 (2026-11-28). Not adopted.** It places nothing new: SIP ledgers and issue labels move when the owner adopts it.
- **The draft start (2026-11-18, #3219)** came from the owner's outside review of the 2.8.1 plan. The owner asked: "Can you update the 2.8.1 plan and draft the start of 2.9 and 2.10 plan accordingly".
- **Revision 1** reads the draft against what 2.9's certification rehearsals found (the pre-registration's amendments 13e to 13g), and against what has been placed since 11-18. It takes the MacBook lane's read of the draft, given on 2026-11-28. **What changed:**
  - item 1 now waits on #3298;
  - items 2 and 3 are aimed at 2.9's recorded limits;
  - three items are added: checks at the proposal seam (10), a compile check of the changed client scripts (11), and the supplied lesson's correction (12);
  - §1 carries what 2.9 sends here;
  - the certification plans a sitting for the cases a person must attend, and is sized from 2.9's timings.

**What 2.10 is.** An even minor: a feature release, led by a headline (CLAUDE.md, #281).
- **What 2.8 showed:** a squad took two increments of an existing Roblox game to accepted source, each proved in a Studio play. Both were text and HUD changes.
- **The review's aim:** a supervised Campaign that can reliably improve a game, rather than one that only runs a sequence of passing cycles.
- **What 2.10 builds:** what a squad needs to propose, verify and repair gameplay changes. It certifies the result with a supervised Campaign.
- **What it needs first:** 2.8.1, then 2.9 (`2-8-1-plan.md`, `2-9-0-plan.md`).

**The claim, drafted:**

> **A supervised Campaign makes three consecutive useful improvements to a private copy of a Roblox game:**
> - at least one changes gameplay, not only displayed text;
> - each is verified by play and replayed against the earlier increments' frozen criteria;
> - a seeded gameplay defect is recovered through the intended path;
> - every failed attempt, return, repair, escalation and intervention is on the record.

## 2. The headline: what a squad needs to improve gameplay (drafted)

Each item says what evidence it gives and what it can't prove.

1. **Play actions that reach a named place or state.**
   - **Today:** `move_to` takes coordinates only, and it can't arrive at all as the reader stands (#3298). The wait reads a path the reader's `resolve` can't walk, so the step reads `setup_failed` on every tree. 2.9's certification returns any proposal that uses it (pre-registration 13e point 7).
   - **Depends on:** #3298's fix, in the first patch after 2.9's tag, with its own requalification.
   - **The change:** a move to a named instance or a declared gameplay state, with evidence that it arrived.
     - The player's position is read within a stated tolerance of the target, in three dimensions, and recorded with the play.
     - A target behind a gameplay transition, such as a teleport that freezes the character, is waited out by a declared reading, not by a fixed sleep.
     - A move that doesn't arrive fails the setup by name.
2. **Readings compared across steps.**
   - **The change:** an observation compared with an earlier step's reading, not only with a constant: changed, unchanged, or increased or decreased by an amount within a band. It's for timers, counters, health and rewards.
   - **Why:** the window's clock could only be checked by its text after a hold. A cross-step reading says the clock advanced by the hold's length, within a band.
   - **The two limits it is aimed at,** both recorded in 2.9:
     - **A label stuck inside the band** passes a single read at a fixed time. The 2.9 blind control's limit, and every approved clock check's.
     - **The band's thin margin.** The clock's zero and the hold's start are offset by the play's setup: about 3 s on 2.9's setup, which left about 2 s at the band's top edge.

     Two readings a known hold apart, compared with each other, are independent of both.
3. **A map of the place for the proposer.**
   - **What it holds:** the reachable locations, the instances a scenario can observe, the attributes the game sets and where, the existing mechanics and the player's actions.
   - **Where it comes from:** the source tree and a Studio read of the place, versioned against the baseline tree.
   - **What reads it:** the proposer is shown it. A deterministic check at the proposal seam also reads it: every path a criterion waits on or observes is on the map, or added by the footprint. That's 2.8.1 item 4's reachability rule, made mechanical.
   - **It carries value types, not only paths:** a state field's type, and the value that names a place.
     - **Why:** in both of 2.9's rehearsals of the certification Campaign, a headless criterion keyed a rule on strings where the game holds a number. It would have passed on a rule the game never calls with those values.
     - **How it was caught:** by a person reading the source, not by any play. A map that carries the type lets the seam check catch it.
4. **Screenshots tied to their play.**
   - **The change:** one per play step, with the source revision, the place pin, the scenario and the session in its record.
   - **Its limit:** a visual review reads them and says what it can't prove. A screenshot is never an assertion.
5. **A headless harness for client scripts.**
   - **The change:** client Luau, such as UI controllers and input handling, tested under Lune with stand-ins for the client services.
   - **Why:** client behaviour gets headless tests where a play can't reach it. 2.8.0's floor half and duration format were proved headless only.
6. **Declared coverage gaps, accepted by a person.**
   - **The change:** where the vocabulary can't prove an effect, the proposal declares the gap in a structured field, and the ruling accepts it explicitly. A weaker check never substitutes.
   - **Today:** 2.8.1 item 4 puts the gap in the proposal's text. This makes it a field the gate reads.
7. **Multiplayer, marked unverified.** A criterion whose effect needs more than one client is a declared gap until a multi-client path exists. That path isn't built in 2.10.
8. **Gameplay increments, not only text.**
   - **What changes:** the squad's skills and the certification's goal set reach a bounded change to a mechanic, interaction, challenge or progression, with a measurable outcome for the player.
   - **Tested by:** the strategy and scenario skills each gain a gameplay example, replayed as 2.8.1 item 4 is.
9. **A supervisor can replay a lesson over the API (#3205).**
   - **The route:** `POST /api/v1/memory/replays` on the `/api/v1` lane, with lesson revisions by id, a held rubric and an immutable experiment reference. A deploy that recreates the agent marks a running replay interrupted.
   - **The scope:** least privilege. Promoting a lesson stays a person's approval (`memory:approve`).
   - **Who rules:** it's an auth-boundary change, so the owner rules on the scope (SIP-0062). A numbered SIP-0110 amendment covers it.
10. **Mechanical checks at the proposal seam,** before a person reads the proposal.
    - **The checks:**
      - a feature proposal with an empty `prd_delta` is refused;
      - the pre-registration's three shapes on an element the footprint adds are refused: a `not_equals` on it, a setup action that needs it, and a precondition on it;
      - a named reading that only repeats its own wait's condition is refused, since it doesn't depend on the time waited.
    - **Why:** every rehearsal of 2.9's certification returned an increment's first proposal on at least one of these, and the ruler's first read missed one (rehearsal 2, amendment 13g). These are rules, so a rule can catch them, and the ruling is left with the judgement.
    - **Where:** beside item 3's path check, as a widening of it.
    - **Its limit:** a check that passes doesn't make a proposal sound. The ruling still reads it.
11. **A compile check of every changed client script in the footprint,** as a build check, before any play.
    - **Why:** in rehearsal 2's case 1, a candidate's client module didn't parse. Every play read `setup_failed`, and the Campaign's repair cycle repeated the fault (#3304). 2.9's fix (#3306) makes the play name the file and line. A compile check catches the fault before a play is spent on it.
    - **Compared with item 5:** cheaper and surer. It checks syntax and types, and item 5's harness checks behaviour.
    - **Taken with it:** #3272, the row-8 repair cycle's prompt carrying the script error.
12. **The supplied lesson corrected (#3297),** by the owner's ruling of 2026-11-27.
    - **The defect:** the one approved lesson was drafted from the two items it's later supplied on, and carries the passing form of one item's check. So a first-pass approval is no evidence that the squad worked the bar out.
    - **The fix:** keep the cases a lesson is drafted and replay-checked on apart from the cases it's evaluated on (SIP-0110, "The cases"), including on a goal set too small to hold them apart.
    - **Why it's before the certification:** §3 tracks "the proposals approved on the first pass", and that figure means nothing until this is fixed.

## 3. The certification: a supervised Campaign on a disposable copy (drafted)

2.10's counted run (D2), pre-registered as 2.8's was:
- **Who supervises:** an API client the operator runs (SIP-0115), not the Spark or MacBook lane. The owner attends.
- **The increments:** three consecutive ones. Each has a stated player outcome, a bounded footprint, acceptance criteria and the supervisor's ruling. At least one changes gameplay behaviour.
- **The checks:**
  - criteria are frozen before development;
  - each named check executes and fails on the baseline, then passes on the candidate;
  - every earlier accepted increment's frozen criteria are replayed on each candidate.
- **A seeded defect:** a seeded, attributable gameplay defect is detected and repaired through the intended path. The pre-registration names which path: 2.9's in-run repair, or §10 row 8.
- **An independent review:** a person who didn't rule the gates plays the result and reads the record. They confirm whether the changes are useful and the game still plays.
- **A planned sitting for the cases that need a person:**
  - **Which cases:** those that read a real person's input at the Mac, such as 2.9's cases 3 and 3b.
  - **Why a sitting:** they can't be stood in for. A declared user activity (`caffeinate -u`) doesn't move the idle counter the worker reads (read on 2026-11-28, amendment 13g).
  - **What the pre-registration names:** the sitting's time, its length, and what's recorded if nobody attends.
- **Sized from 2.9's timings:**
  - **One-increment Campaign:** 25 to 40 min (2.9's rehearsals).
  - **Two-increment Campaign:** 80 to 90 min, with one return at each increment's gate.
  - **Three increments with a seeded defect:** budgeted at 3 to 4 h, plus returns, a repair cycle, and the independent review's play.
  - **Unattended:** the window runs without the owner (the owner, 2026-11-28: "I need you guys to run autonomously!"). Nothing in it waits on an owner's answer. In 2.9, a window closed unused while a question waited.
- **The record:** every failed attempt, return, repair, escalation, timeout and intervention.
- **What's never allowed:**
  - no hand edits, no silent requirement change and no unapproved scope;
  - nothing published to a live experience. Accepted work stays on the Campaign's branch until the owner approves it.

**What's tracked:**
- accepted useful increments, against those attempted;
- the proposals approved on the first pass;
- baseline discrimination and broken-build detection;
- whether repairs succeeded, and whether escalations were correct;
- end-to-end time, and Studio plays per accepted increment;
- regressions, unverified coverage and unresolved gaps;
- interventions by the supervisor, and by a person;
- the improvements a player can see, as shown.

**One Campaign isn't a reliability claim.** Largely unattended operation waits for further independent Campaigns, each pre-registered, with at least one on a second game (D3).
